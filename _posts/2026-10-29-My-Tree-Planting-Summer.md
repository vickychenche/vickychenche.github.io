# My Tree Planting Summer

It's been a while since I've written here. A lot has happened since my last post, and not all of it involved writing code.

Last summer, I stepped away from software engineering to do something I'd wanted to try since I was 13: tree planting.

I guess it's not every day that a kid says, "I want to be a tree planter." But I did. Ever since I was 13, I'd see these badass women covered head to toe in dirt, carrying bags of trees on their hips, and think: I want to be one of them.

Years later, a friend offered to help me get into the industry. It was something I'd dreamed about for so long, and I wasn't about to let the opportunity pass me by. So I left my job as a product engineer and headed west to spend my summer planting trees.

In my mind, it was a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity. And it turned out to be one hell of a summer.

Every workday started at 6 a.m., and I'd usually get home around 7 p.m. It was brutal, exhausting, and somehow, I'd never been happier. I loved the long drives out to the land, the endless stretches of wilderness, and those quiet hours where it was just me and the forest.

I kept a log of every single day I planted. Between May 1 and June 29, I planted on 44 days, putting a total of 41,000 trees in the ground.

In May, I averaged around 685 trees a day. By June, that number had climbed to approximately 1,200. May 22 marked my first 1,000-tree day, with 1,065 trees planted. My personal record came on June 19, when I managed to plant 2,010 trees in a single day.

Of course, not every day was a record-breaking one. There were days when I planted just 375 trees, and others when I barely made it past 600. But that's part of the story, too. Every day was different, and every tree counted.

Looking back at the numbers, it's interesting to see how much my production changed over the season. What felt like small improvements from one day to the next eventually added up to something much bigger.

## What This Summer Gave Me

Tree planting is far from an easy job, but there's something incredibly fulfilling about it. The physical exhaustion, the challenges, and the satisfaction of looking back at a piece of land knowing you've planted every tree yourself.

The season itself only lasts about two months, but the community and friendships I found along the way are something I'll carry with me for much longer.

![My friends and I at the cache, sitting on and around boxes of trees]({{ "/images/planting-crew.jpg" | relative_url }})

*My friends and I at the cache, picking up more trees to plant.*

More than anything, this summer changed how I see myself. I came into the season not knowing what to expect, and left with a newfound confidence in what I'm capable of. I learned that I'm capable of pushing through things I once thought were impossible.

If there's one thing I'm taking away from this experience, it's that I'm a lot tougher than I give myself credit for.

## Unexpected Lessons from Planting Trees

One of the hardest parts of tree planting, and something I never expected going in, was land management.

It wasn't just about digging a hole and putting a tree in the ground. It was about knowing where you were, where you'd already been, and where you needed to go next. It required a level of spatial awareness that I had never really needed before.

It took me a long time to develop that skill. I would constantly get lost, forget where I'd planted my last tree, or lose track of the line I was supposed to be following. All that backtracking wasted valuable time and brought my daily production down.

It wasn't something I could learn overnight. It took repetition, patience, and a whole lot of trial and error.

There were plenty of days when I'd find myself crying out on the land, frustrated because I just couldn't seem to get it right. But I was stubborn. I refused to give up, and I refused to accept that I was simply bad at it.

![Me covered in dirt, in my orange vest with my shovel, on the land]({{ "/images/planting-shovel-selfie.jpg" | relative_url }})

*Me covered in dirt.*

So I kept going. Day after day, I practised, made mistakes, and slowly figured things out.

Eventually, something clicked. I started finding my rhythm, navigating the land more confidently, and spending less time figuring out where I was supposed to be.

Looking back, it was one of the most unexpected skills I took away from the season. I went into tree planting thinking the hardest part would be the physical work. I didn't expect that learning how to navigate the land would challenge me just as much.

## 41,000 Trees Later

I went into the season with a dream I'd had since I was 13. I came out of it with 41,000 trees planted, a lot of dirt under my nails, some incredible friendships, and a better understanding of what I'm capable of.

Would I do it again? Absolutely.

Somewhere between the early mornings, the long drives, the blisters, the frustrating days, and the rookie 2,000-tree day, I found something I didn't even know I was looking for.

Sometimes it's nice to step away from the screen and do something completely different.
